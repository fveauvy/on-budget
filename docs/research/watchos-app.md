## Question

What should the on-budget Apple Watch app do, and how is it built and fed with data? This note covers:

- features and existing examples;
- companion versus independent app, data paths, budgets, storage, auth and privacy;
- SwiftUI and watchOS constraints, next to a phone app that isn't native (ADR 0002), including the iPhone side of the relay;
- sharing code and design tokens;
- how the design system maps to the watch;
- a phased scope.

## TL;DR

1. **The watch app checks and confirms. It isn't a second budgeting app.**
   - **MVP:**
     - the safe-to-spend figure, for you and for shared money, with the daily budget, the days left in the period and pace;
     - complications and a Smart Stack widget;
     - actionable alerts forwarded from the iPhone;
     - a one-tap review inbox ("Monoprix −23,40 € → Courses?").
   - **Later:** cash entry through App Intents and a Control, an App Shortcut phrase, relevance near shops, an interactive widget, and goals.
   - **Never on the wrist:** allocation, settings, bank links, splits. **Medium-high.**
2. **Feed the watch with one small glance that the server builds and the iPhone relays.** It is a versioned read model of a few KB, stamped with the instance's sync cursor. `sync-engine.md` keeps one counter per instance, so a copy replaces the shown one only if its cursor is higher. **Medium-high.**
   - **MVP, every instance: the WatchConnectivity (WC) relay.** The iPhone sends `updateApplicationContext` after every change and, on watchOS 27, `transferCurrentComplicationUserInfo` while a complication is on the face. It answers the watch's `sendMessage` at once from its cached glance. The watch holds no credentials and never calls the server.
   - **Later, direct mode,** only for instances the watch can reach on its own, such as a self-hoster's public one: the watch enrolls through the iPhone, fetches `GET /v1/glance` and takes WidgetKit pushes.
3. **Why the relay first** (**high** on the facts):
   - The reference instance is reachable only over Tailscale (ADR 0003), and Tailscale ships no watch app: its listing covers iPhone, iPad, Vision Pro and Apple TV ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/tailscale/id1470499037)). Over Wi-Fi or cellular the watch can't reach the tailnet. Whether its requests proxied through the iPhone use the iPhone's tunnel is undocumented (spike W3), and WC covers that case anyway (judgment).
   - It goes against Apple's advice. Data from a paired iPhone "can't be your app's primary way of accessing data", and WC is "an opportunistic optimization, rather than the primary means of supplying fresh data" ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/watchos-apps/keeping-your-watchos-app-s-content-up-to-date)). So the watch is only as fresh as the iPhone's last relay, and every surface shows its age.
   - Direct-mode pieces do nothing for this instance. A WidgetKit push only reloads timelines, the widget extension can't reach the server, and "WatchConnectivity is not an option for updating widgets" (an Apple frameworks engineer, [forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/745581), secondary). An enrolled watch would hold a token it can't use.
   - Complication transfers, the relay's fast lane to the face, work with WidgetKit complications only from iOS 27 and watchOS 27 ([iOS 27 RN](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/ios-ipados-release-notes/ios-ipados-27-release-notes), [watchOS 27 RN](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/watchos-release-notes/watchos-27-release-notes)), hence the floor in item 8.
   - **Placement sets freshness.** Complication transfers get 50 a day while a complication is on the active face, otherwise 0 ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/watchconnectivity/wcsession/remainingcomplicationuserinfotransfers)), and watchOS gives background time first to "apps with a complication in the active watch face and apps in the dock" ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/watchos-apps/keeping-your-watchos-app-s-content-up-to-date)). A Smart Stack widget viewed once a day "might get a refresh once a day" ([WWDC26-8014](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2026/8014/)). Onboarding should suggest a face complication.
4. **On the iPhone, the relay is native Swift inside the React Native app** (**medium**; the facts **high**):
   - A `WatchRelay` Expo module owns `WCSession`, activated at launch through an AppDelegate subscriber. It answers from native code, because iOS may wake the app for the watch with no JavaScript running.
   - It fetches `GET /v1/glance` itself, through the Tailscale tunnel: on a watch request, a "glance changed" background push, its own `BGAppRefreshTask`, or a hint from the JS sync client. The JS client is a poor background source: `expo-background-task` schedules a `BGProcessingTask` ([source](https://github.com/expo/expo/blob/5df0528eed6c96c1fb2f80787e944df08071a62a/packages/expo-background-task/ios/BackgroundTaskScheduler.swift#L92-L95)), and Expo warns that iOS "typically runs background tasks during specific windows, such as overnight" ([source](https://github.com/expo/expo/blob/841fece292389b45e7be3b8672544eba2ba43c60/packages/expo-background-task/src/BackgroundTask.types.ts#L40-L43)).
   - Watch writes and notification "Confirm" actions go to a native relay client with its own client ID, outbox and contiguous mutation IDs, which posts to `/push`. The JS app's outbox keeps its single writer, as `sync-engine.md` requires.
   - While Tailscale is enabled, its client keeps an automatic On Demand policy so the tunnel survives restarts and crashes ([Tailscale](https://tailscale.com/docs/features/client/ios-vpn-on-demand)). Turning Tailscale off, another VPN, or a custom rule that disconnects it stalls the relay, and the watch then shows its age.
5. **Don't run the sync engine on the watch.** **High** on the facts; the conclusion is judgment.
   - Background refresh runs about 4 times per hour, only with a complication on the active face, and for "a few seconds" each time ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/watchkit/wkapplicationrefreshbackgroundtask)). That is too little to bootstrap and pull two spaces (judgment).
   - Where the watch can reach the server, networking isn't the blocker. `/pull` and `/push` are plain HTTPS, which watchOS allows; only the WebSocket poke is blocked, as low-level networking ([TN3135](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/technotes/tn3135-low-level-networking-on-watchos)), and APNs could replace it. Budgets, storage, battery and a third client implementation are the reasons.
   - Writes from the watch reuse the same named mutations: through the iPhone's relay client in the MVP, and posted by the watch as its own client in direct mode.
6. **Alerts keep targeting the iPhone.** **High**; decryption of forwarded notifications still needs checking.
   - iOS forwards them to the watch when the phone is locked and the watch is worn.
   - Background actions run on the target device, the iPhone, so the watch needs no credentials for them. Double tap runs the first non-destructive action, so put "Confirm" first ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/watchos-apps/adding-actions-to-notifications-on-watchos)).
   - **Trade-off:** a cellular watch whose iPhone is off or offline gets no alerts; its review screen still works from the cached glance and queues actions. Sending to both devices needs identical payloads, which per-device encryption rules out (§2.6).
   - With "hide amounts" on, notification text carries no merchant or amount. The HIG says to "avoid including sensitive, personal, or confidential information in a notification" ([HIG](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/notifications)).
7. **Privacy on the wrist** (**high** on the facts, **medium** on the defaults):
   - In the app, `privacySensitive` hides amounts in Always On, where Apple says to "always hide … financial information" ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/watchos-apps/designing-your-app-for-the-always-on-state)).
   - In complications, `privacySensitive` defers to watch settings that show the data by default (an Apple DTS engineer, [forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/806236), secondary). So the complication views hide amounts themselves when `isLuminanceReduced` is true.
   - Offer a "hide amounts on the watch" option.
   - Rely on the watch passcode and wrist detection. The watch has no biometric policy ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/localauthentication/lapolicy/deviceownerauthenticationwithbiometrics)), so this is an exception to `PRODUCT.md`'s "biometric-locked" principle, for the owner to approve.
   - Optionally hide whole complications while the watch is locked, with the Data Protection entitlement.
8. **Build:**
   - A single-target SwiftUI watch app plus a WidgetKit extension, as Swift targets next to the Expo iOS app, with a **watchOS 27.0 floor** if the household's watches allow it. watchOS 27 runs on SE 3, Series 9–12 and Ultra 2–4, paired with an iPhone on iOS 27 ([Apple](https://www.apple.com/os/watchos/)). All of them have double tap and wrist flick, and complication transfers work there. A 26.0 floor would keep Series 6–8, SE 2 and Ultra 1 ([watchOS 26](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2025/06/watchos-26-delivers-more-personalized-ways-to-stay-active-and-connected/)), whose complications would refresh only when the watch app runs (§3.2).
   - The phone app shares no code with the watch, so the server-built glance is the only contract. The phone side adds the `WatchRelay` module (item 4). `cross-platform-ui-stack.md` §4.7 commits the Expo app's `ios/` project, where the watch targets are ordinary Xcode targets; `@bacons/apple-targets` would generate them under Continuous Native Generation (CNG), but its watch support has open blockers (§3.3).
   - Swift packages are shared only among the watch app, its widget extension and the iPhone's Swift code (`WatchRelay`, widgets, notification service), plus the server if it is Swift.
   - **High** on the floor facts, **medium-high** on the pick, **medium** on the package split.
9. **The design system needs a watch profile** (**medium**):
   - the same semantic tokens, generated by Terrazzo through the `platform: watchos` context in [`design-system.md`](design-system.md);
   - dark only, since watchOS has no Dark Mode setting ([HIG](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/dark-mode));
   - SF Compact text styles (16 pt default, 12 pt minimum; SF Compact Rounded in complications) ([HIG](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/typography));
   - semantic icon keys, drawn with SF Symbols on Apple and Lucide on the web;
   - scene padding;
   - materials instead of elevation;
   - status never shown by color alone, because complications can be tinted.

## 1. What the watch should do

### 1.1 Jobs on the wrist

Apple says people use a watch app's complications, notifications and Siri "more than they use the app itself", in interactions that last "less than a minute" ([HIG](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/designing-for-watchos)). This matches PRODUCT.md's daily loop: check what's left, then review new spending.

| Job | Moment | Surface | Content |
|---|---|---|---|
| "Can I buy this?" | At a shop, before paying | Complication, Smart Stack, app home | Safe-to-spend (you or shared), daily budget, J-N left in the period (to month end or the next payday, still open in `PRODUCT.md`), pace |
| "Am I near a limit?" | After an alert | Notification, app | Category progress, threshold or pace crossed |
| "Confirm this spend" | Daily ritual, or when a bank line arrives | Actionable notification, review list | Merchant, amount, the top 2–3 suggested categories, as in Copilot's "top two guesses first" ([`budgeting-products.md`](budgeting-products.md)) |
| "Log cash" | Paying cash (later) | App Intent, Siri, Control | Amount, category |

Freshness has a ceiling. Without the user present, the server can fetch each bank account at most 4 times a day ([RTS art. 36(5)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32018R0389), via [`bank-aggregation.md`](bank-aggregation.md)). So the watch needs updates a few times a day plus right after the user acts, not real time (judgment).

## 2. Architecture

### 2.1 Companion or independent

| Model | Apple's rules | Fit |
|---|---|---|
| **Dependent companion** (the default for "Watch App with New Companion iOS App") | Can't be launched until the iOS app is installed ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/watchos-apps/creating-independent-watchos-apps)) | **The install model.** Onboarding, bank consent and passkeys stay on the iPhone |
| **Independent with companion** (`WKRunsIndependentlyOfCompanionApp`) ([key](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/bundleresources/information-property-list/wkrunsindependentlyofcompanionapp)) | Must "create new accounts and sign in", download data itself, and receive pushes directly. It "can't use Watch Connectivity as its main source of data" ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/watchos-apps/creating-independent-watchos-apps)) | Ruled out in relay mode, whose main source is WC. Direct mode (later) behaves like this at runtime, alerts excepted (§2.6). Independent installation isn't needed |
| **Watch-only** | No iOS app | No |

**Pick:** ship a companion, with the watch's bundle ID's companion key (`WKCompanionAppBundleIdentifier`) set to the iOS bundle ID ([key](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/bundleresources/information-property-list/wkcompanionappbundleidentifier)). Keep install independence off. In relay mode, the MVP, the watch shows its cached glance and queues writes without the iPhone, but gets fresh data only through it. Direct mode later removes that dependency on instances the watch can reach. **High.**

### 2.2 Data paths

| | **WatchConnectivity (WC) relay through the iPhone (MVP, every instance)** | **Direct HTTPS (later, instances the watch can reach)** | Push to the watch carrying an encrypted glance (later option) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reaching the server | The watch never calls it. The iPhone reaches a Tailscale-only instance through its tunnel (§2.4) | Needs an instance reachable from the internet, so not the reference one: Tailscale has no watch app ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/tailscale/id1470499037)), and whether traffic the iPhone proxies uses its VPN is unverified (W3) | Not needed: APNs is outbound from the server, so it fits a Tailscale-only instance |
| Fresh when the iPhone app isn't running | When iOS wakes it: a watch `sendMessage`, a background push, or `BGAppRefreshTask` (§2.3) | Yes, through the watch's refresh budget and WidgetKit push | Partly: background pushes "two or three per hour" at most, not guaranteed ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/usernotifications/pushing-background-updates-to-your-app)). PushKit `complication` pushes allow 50 a day, only while a complication is on the face ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/pushkit/pkpushtype/complication)); an Apple engineer says the system then updates complications "and not necessarily give[s] your app runtime" ([forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/757587), secondary) |
| Phone out of range (cellular watch) | Stale, with its age shown | Fresh | Fresh |
| Credentials on the watch | None | Device token and Secure Enclave key, shared with the widget extension (§2.7) | A per-watch payload key and an APNs token, relayed by the phone |
| Writes | `sendMessage`, or `transferUserInfo` when unreachable, to the iPhone's relay client (§2.5) | `POST /push` as its own client | Relay |
| Simulator testing | Partial: `transferUserInfo` and complication transfers need paired devices ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/watchconnectivity/wcsession/transferuserinfo(_:))); background tasks need a physical iPhone ([Expo](https://docs.expo.dev/versions/latest/sdk/background-task/)) | Yes | Device |
| Apple's position | "Opportunistic optimization" | Preferred | Low priority |
| Extra server work | The glance endpoint and "glance changed" background pushes to the iPhone | Enrollment and widget push tokens | Key management; payloads of at most 4 KB ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/usernotifications/sending-notification-requests-to-apns)) |

**Pick:** the relay in the MVP, for every instance. Direct mode later, only where the owner marks the instance as reachable from the watch, a setting the iPhone passes on (judgment). Keep the third column as a later option for cellular watches away from their iPhone, since it needs no reachable server. **High** on the reference instance's constraint, **medium-high** on the split.

### 2.3 Budgets and limits

| Mechanism | Budget or limit |
|---|---|
| Background app refresh | "Approximately four tasks per hour for each app with a complication on the active watch face", shared by its complications ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/watchkit/wkapplicationrefreshbackgroundtask)). It needs that complication, gives "a few seconds", and runs one scheduled task at a time ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/watchkit/using-background-tasks), [Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/watchkit/wkapplication/schedulebackgroundrefresh(withpreferreddate:userinfo:scheduledcompletion:))). It brings no data in relay mode: the watch can't call the server, and `sendMessage` from the background needs "a high priority" such as a workout ([isReachable](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/watchconnectivity/wcsession/isreachable)) |
| WC background tasks (watch) | "Triggered whenever the paired device sends data" through application context, user info, complication info or a file ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/watchkit/wkwatchconnectivityrefreshbackgroundtask)). Background time goes first to apps with a face complication or in the dock (TL;DR 3). In a 2025 report, complications reloaded from such a task stopped updating on watchOS 11.5 unless WidgetKit Developer Mode was on (secondary, [forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/788014)) |
| WidgetKit reloads | Per-widget daily budget, "typically … 40 to 70 refreshes" for a widget viewed often, with entries at least about 5 min apart. Taps on app intents and the foreground app don't count ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/widgetkit/keeping-a-widget-up-to-date)). A complication gets "up to 75 updates per day", and one on the face is "always considered viewed" ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/widgetkit/converting-a-clockkit-app)) |
| Placement | Apple engineers: a widget can update "every 15 to 20 minutes if it's being actively used"; face complications are "at the top tier of the budget"; a Smart Stack widget's refresh "depends on how often the user actually goes to look at your widget", so one viewed once a day "might get a refresh once a day" ([WWDC26-8014](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2026/8014/)) |
| `transferCurrentComplicationUserInfo` (iPhone to watch) | 50 per day while the complication is on the active face, otherwise 0, after which calls fall back to `transferUserInfo` ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/watchconnectivity/wcsession/remainingcomplicationuserinfotransfers)); with `isComplicationEnabled` false they "fail immediately" ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/watchconnectivity/wcsession/iscomplicationenabled)). It is "transmitted right away" and "counts against your complication's time budget" ([WCSession](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/watchconnectivity/wcsession)). A Smart Stack widget isn't a complication for WC (forum [745581](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/745581), secondary). It didn't work with WidgetKit complications until the iOS 27 and watchOS 27 fix ([iOS 27 RN](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/ios-ipados-release-notes/ios-ipados-27-release-notes), [watchOS 27 RN](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/watchos-release-notes/watchos-27-release-notes)); an Apple frameworks engineer had confirmed the bug in 2024 ([forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/759389), secondary) |
| `updateApplicationContext` | A dictionary where the latest version wins, sent "when the opportunity arises", and "well suited for updating your app's glance" ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/watchconnectivity/wcsession/updateapplicationcontext(_:))). Oversized payloads fail with `payloadTooLarge`; the limit isn't stated on the pages I read ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/watchconnectivity/wcerror/code/payloadtoolarge)) |
| `sendMessage` | Requires `isReachable`, which on the watch means the app is in the foreground or at high priority in the background ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/watchconnectivity/wcsession/isreachable)). Sent from the watch, it "wakes up the corresponding iOS app in the background"; sent from iOS, it doesn't wake the watch ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/watchconnectivity/wcsession/sendmessage(_:replyhandler:errorhandler:))). A reply that comes too late fails with `messageReplyTimedOut`, and the timeout isn't documented ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/watchconnectivity/wcerror/code/messagereplytimedout)) |
| iPhone background time | `BGAppRefreshTask`: when the system decides, "up to 30 seconds"; `BGProcessingTask`: work the system defers to "periods of low activity, such as overnight when the device charges" ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/backgroundtasks/choosing-background-strategies-for-your-app)). A background push is low priority, should come at most "two or three per hour" ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/usernotifications/pushing-background-updates-to-your-app)) and gets 30 s, but the system "does not automatically launch your app if the user has force-quit it" ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/uikit/uiapplicationdelegate/application(_:didreceiveremotenotification:fetchcompletionhandler:))) |
| WidgetKit push (direct mode) | watchOS 26+ ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/widgetkit/widgetpushhandler)). `apns-push-type: widgets`, topic `<bundleID>.push-type.widgets`, `content-changed: true`. Budgeted, "delivered opportunistically", no broadcast channels ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/widgetkit/updating-widgets-with-widgetkit-push-notifications)). Covers complications ([WWDC25-334](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/334/)) |
| Background push to the watch | Needs `apns-push-type`; low priority; send at most "two or three per hour" ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/usernotifications/pushing-background-updates-to-your-app)); 30 s to handle ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/watchkit/wkapplicationdelegate/didreceiveremotenotification(_:fetchcompletionhandler:))) |
| Networking | `URLSession` HTTP(S) only. Network framework, `URLSessionWebSocketTask` and BSD sockets are blocked; `NWPathMonitor` stays `.unsatisfied`; `Data(contentsOf:)` with network URLs is unsupported ([TN3135](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/technotes/tn3135-low-level-networking-on-watchos)) |

### 2.4 Proposed data flow (relay mode)

```
 bank sync ─▶ server: ingestion → mutators → change_log → GlanceBuilder
                 │ APNs: alerts and "glance changed" pushes       ▲
                 ▼                                                │ HTTPS through the iPhone's Tailscale tunnel
 iPhone, Expo app                                                 │
   JS UI + JS sync client: main database and its outbox ──────────┤ /bootstrap /pull /push
   WatchRelay (Swift): WCSession, glance cache, relay client ─────┘ GET /v1/glance, POST /push
   NSE (Swift): decrypts alerts; iOS forwards them to the watch
                 │ updateApplicationContext · transferCurrentComplicationUserInfo (27) · replies
                 ▼                                    ▲ sendMessage · transferUserInfo (actions)
 watch app ─▶ app group: snapshot.json, pending.json ─▶ widget extension (reads only)
```

- **GlanceBuilder** is a pure function on the server, the source of truth, written in the server's language; the watch only decodes its output. It reads one REPEATABLE READ snapshot of the member's spaces and stamps the counter value read in it, as `/pull` does ([`sync-engine.md`](sync-engine.md)). The phone could later rebuild the glance in TypeScript to show unacknowledged edits (`pending > 0`, `"source": "phone"`): the same function if the server is TypeScript, as [`web-client-sync.md`](web-client-sync.md) now recommends, and golden JSON vectors pin what the watch decodes. The MVP relays server glances only (judgment).
- **The iPhone side:** `WatchRelay`, a Swift module in the Expo app (judgment on top of the cited facts).
  - **Start.** An `ExpoAppDelegateSubscriber` ([Expo](https://docs.expo.dev/modules/appdelegate-subscribers/)) activates `WCSession` in `didFinishLaunching` and registers the module's `BGAppRefreshTask`: all launch handlers "must be complete before the end of" that method ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/backgroundtasks/bgtaskscheduler/register(fortaskwithidentifier:using:launchhandler:))). The delegate implements the activation callbacks, or the app "opts out of multiple Apple Watch support" ([WCSession](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/watchconnectivity/wcsession)).
  - **No JavaScript needed.** React Native's runtime may not be running when iOS wakes the app (§3.3), so the module answers `sendMessage` from its cache and fetches natively.
  - **Who refreshes the glance.** The module fetches `GET /v1/glance` with `URLSession` and relays it: on a watch request when its copy is older than 2 min; on a "glance changed" background push, `sync-engine.md`'s best-effort refresh hint, which the server coalesces to stay under Apple's two or three an hour (§2.3; judgment); in its own `BGAppRefreshTask`; and when the JS sync client reports, after a pull or push, a cursor ahead of the cached glance's. The JS client can't stand in for it reliably in the background: `expo-background-task` 58 submits a `BGProcessingTaskRequest` ([source](https://github.com/expo/expo/blob/5df0528eed6c96c1fb2f80787e944df08071a62a/packages/expo-background-task/ios/BackgroundTaskScheduler.swift#L92-L95)), which iOS keeps for quiet periods (§2.3).
  - **Tokens.** The module takes access tokens from the `Security` Swift module of [`cross-platform-ui-stack.md`](cross-platform-ui-stack.md) §4.8, which holds the Secure Enclave DPoP key and refreshes tokens natively, so JS and the relay share one refresher. Two refreshers would race, and reusing a rotated refresh token revokes the device ([`native-apps-auth.md`](native-apps-auth.md), Recommendation 3).
  - **The tunnel.** The VPN runs in Tailscale's network extension, apart from on-budget's process, so background requests use it whenever it is up (judgment). While Tailscale is enabled, its client sets "a broad VPN On Demand policy … to ensure that the VPN remains active in the event of a system restart, auto-update, crash or other event"; disabling Tailscale removes it, custom rules replace it, and connecting another VPN disables it "until you manually connect Tailscale again" ([Tailscale](https://tailscale.com/docs/features/client/ios-vpn-on-demand), validated 2026-01-05). Apple's default keeps a VPN connected while the device sleeps ([`disconnectOnSleep`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/networkextension/nevpnprotocol/disconnectonsleep)).
  - So the tunnel is up in the common case. It is down when the member turns Tailscale off, uses another VPN, or sets a rule such as "Except On" home Wi-Fi, which cuts a VPS reachable only over Tailscale. Fetches then fail fast, the relay keeps its outbox and last glance, the watch shows the age, and the iPhone app warns after a day without reaching the instance (judgment; spike W3).
- **On watch launch:**
  1. Render the cached snapshot immediately, with "il y a 12 min" ("12 min ago") through `Text(date, style: .relative)` ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/widgetkit/displaying-dynamic-dates)).
  2. If the iPhone is reachable, flush pending actions and send `sendMessage(.refresh)`. The iPhone replies at once from its cache, then fetches if its copy is older than 2 min and sends the result through `updateApplicationContext`. Replying only after the fetch would risk `messageReplyTimedOut` (§2.3).
  3. Otherwise show the age and a "phone unreachable" state; the review screen still queues actions (judgment).
- **On iPhone changes:** call `updateApplicationContext(envelope)` after every new glance, including in background tasks, a use Apple names ([WCSession](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/watchconnectivity/wcsession)). On watchOS 27, when a complication is on the active face and what it shows changes, also send `transferCurrentComplicationUserInfo`.
- **On the watch:** each WC delivery wakes the app in the background (§2.3). It writes `snapshot.json` to the app group and reloads the widget timelines only if what they show changed, to spare the reload budget (judgment). The widget extension never fetches; its timelines read the snapshot.
- **Timelines:** pre-compute future entries for date-driven changes such as midnight (daily budget) and the period end, so no reload is needed (judgment).
- **Ordering copies.** `sync-engine.md` keeps one sync counter per instance, and one `/pull` covers all of a member's spaces from one snapshot. So the glance carries a single `cursor`: the counter value of the snapshot it was built from.
  - A copy replaces the shown one only if its cursor is higher. Equal cursors mean the same server state, so keep the shown copy. A phone-side rebuild, if added, would break ties with a newer `seq` from the same source.
  - Never compare `computedAt` across devices, because the iPhone's and the server's clocks differ. It only drives the "as of" label (judgment).
- **Direct mode (later).** WidgetKit "reloads your timelines" on a push whose payload carries only `content-changed` ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/widgetkit/updating-widgets-with-widgetkit-push-notifications)). So the widget extension fetches `GET /v1/glance` itself, with the watch's credentials in a keychain access group it shares with the watch app. The two must not rotate the same refresh token concurrently, or reuse detection ([`apple-client-stack.md`](apple-client-stack.md) §3.3) revokes the watch: give the extension its own `glance:read` token, or serialize refreshes with a lock in the app group (judgment).

The glance in v1 is a few KB (judgment). Amounts are in minor units, as in `sync-engine.md`. Over WC, an envelope wraps it with the relay's acknowledgments (§2.5).

```jsonc
{ "v": 1, "source": "server", "seq": 412, "computedAt": "2026-11-07T18:02:11Z",
  "cursor": 27354, "pending": 0,
  "currency": "EUR", "tz": "Europe/Paris", "periodEnd": "2026-11-30",
  "scopes": [{ "id": "me", "safe": 41250, "perDay": 1719, "pace": 0.93, "status": "ok" },
             { "id": "shared", "safe": 68000, "perDay": 2833, "pace": 1.08, "status": "watch" }],
  "limits": [{ "cat": "c-groceries", "spent": 32000, "limit": 40000, "status": "warn" }],
  "inbox": { "count": 3, "items": [{ "id": "t-…", "merchant": "Monoprix", "amount": -2340,
             "date": "2026-11-07", "suggest": ["c-groceries", "c-restaurants"] }] },
  "categories": [{ "id": "c-groceries", "systemKey": "groceries", "name": null, "icon": "groceries", "color": "cat.green" }],
  "prefs": { "hideAmounts": false, "region": null, "hourCycle": "h23", "firstDayOfWeek": null } }
```

- **Categories.** A built-in category travels as a `systemKey` that the watch renders from its own string catalog; only a renamed category carries a `name` ([`i18n-l10n.md`](i18n-l10n.md) §6).
- **Icons.** `icon` is a semantic key, mapped through `icons.yaml` to an SF Symbol on Apple and a Lucide icon on the web ([`design-system.md`](design-system.md) §2.8). Apple licenses its system symbols "solely for the purpose of developing Applications for Apple-branded products" ([Xcode and Apple SDKs Agreement §2.10](https://www.apple.com/legal/sla/docs/xcode.pdf)), so a symbol name can't be the contract.
- The same read model can feed the iOS widgets and the shared-UI apps (judgment).

### 2.5 Writes from the watch

- Use the same versioned named mutations as everywhere else: `categorize@1`, `markReviewed@1`, later `addManual@1`. Inserts carry a client UUIDv7 ([`sync-engine.md`](sync-engine.md)), and every watch action carries its own UUIDv7 `actionId` and tap time (judgment).
- **Transport (relay mode).** Use `sendMessage` when the iPhone is reachable: it wakes the iPhone app and gives an immediate acknowledgment. Otherwise use `transferUserInfo`, which is queued and "ensure[s] that it's delivered" ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/watchconnectivity/wcsession/transferuserinfo(_:))); whether iOS launches the iPhone app to deliver it isn't documented (W1). The watch keeps each action in `pending.json` until it is acknowledged.
- **The relay client on the iPhone.** `sync-engine.md` gives each local database one writer, one client ID and contiguous mutation IDs, so a Swift module can't append to the JS app's outbox.
  - `cross-platform-ui-stack.md` §4.4 takes the other way out for App Intents captures: a handoff file in the app group that the JS client drains, accepting that a capture reaches the server "only at the phone app's next sync". In the background JS runs only in `BGProcessingTask` windows (§2.3). For the watch's review that is too slow: the server glance keeps listing the transaction, the watch keeps it pending, and the partner sees it unreviewed (judgment).
  - So watch actions and "Confirm" go to a relay client of its own instead (judgment):
    - its own small database holds a `clientId`, an outbox, a mutation counter and an HLC, a Swift port of `sync-engine.md`'s clock that stamps each action from the watch's tap time, capped at the iPhone's clock;
    - it drops a duplicate `actionId`, since a timed-out `sendMessage` and its `transferUserInfo` fallback can deliver one action twice;
    - it sends `POST /push {clientId, mutations[]}` inside `beginBackgroundTask` ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/uikit/uiapplication/beginbackgroundtask(withname:expirationhandler:))) and retries from its outbox on the next wake;
    - it runs no mutators: the server validates, and the JS app sees the result on its next pull;
    - its outbox file stays readable while the iPhone is locked, because "users can respond to actions while the device is locked", when files with complete protection are unavailable ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/usernotifications/declaring-your-actionable-notification-types)).
- **Acknowledgments.** `/push` returns `{mutationId, syncId}` or `{mutationId, rejected, reason}` for each mutation ([`sync-engine.md`](sync-engine.md)). The relay replies to `sendMessage` with that result when it comes in time. Since an application context keeps only the latest dictionary, every envelope also lists the watch actions received in the last 24 h as `queued`, `{syncId}` or `{rejected, reason}` (judgment).
- **Optimistic view.** The watch shows the result at once and keeps the overlay until a glance's `cursor` reaches the action's `syncId`, `sync-engine.md`'s "wait on sync" rule. A rejection drops the overlay and shows the reason.
- **Direct mode (later):** the watch is its own client with the same outbox rules, posting to `/push` and retrying on the next launch or refresh.

### 2.6 Notifications

- **Routing.** If the iPhone is unlocked with its screen on, the alert goes to the iPhone. Otherwise, if the watch is worn and unlocked, it goes to the watch. Otherwise it falls back to the iPhone. Background pushes are never forwarded ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/watchos-apps/taking-advantage-of-notification-forwarding)). For a dependent app, sending to the iPhone alone is enough ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/watchos-apps/enabling-and-receiving-notifications)).
- **Actions.** Categories come from the iOS app, and "the system shares the actionable notification types already registered by your iPhone app" ([HIG](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/notifications)).
  - Foreground actions run where they are tapped.
  - Background actions run on "the device that was the notification's target", so a forwarded "Confirm" runs on the iPhone ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/watchos-apps/adding-actions-to-notifications-on-watchos)).
  - **On the iPhone under Expo.** `expo-notifications` makes a singleton the notification center's delegate, forwards each response to native delegates registered with `addDelegate`, then calls the completion handler as soon as they return ([source](https://github.com/expo/expo/blob/e922b0b69bb8d84610826735d466d6eaa3163281/packages/expo-notifications/ios/ExpoNotifications/Notifications/NotificationCenterManager.swift#L225-L238)). So `WatchRelay` registers a delegate at launch, hands "Confirm" to the relay client (§2.5), and holds its own background task while it posts (judgment).
  - **Design:** `Confirm <suggestion>` (background, listed first so double tap triggers it), `Change…` (foreground, opens the watch review screen), `Later`.
- **Without the iPhone.** Forwarding starts from the iPhone. So a cellular watch whose iPhone is off or offline gets no alert and can't run `Confirm`; the review screen still works from the cached glance and queues its actions (§2.5).
  - Apple tells independent apps to "always send the notification to both iPhone and Apple Watch" ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/watchos-apps/enabling-and-receiving-notifications)). But the system drops the duplicate only "as long as the payloads are identical" ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/watchkit/wkextension/registerforremotenotifications())), and per-device encryption makes them differ.
  - Accept the gap in the MVP. Later, the iPhone could relay the watch's APNs token to the server and hand the watch the same payload key, so both payloads match. APNs is outbound, so this works on a Tailscale-only instance (judgment; **spike W2**).
- **Content.** The HIG says to "avoid including sensitive, personal, or confidential information in a notification". On the watch, short looks should "avoid including potentially sensitive information in the notification's title" ([HIG](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/notifications)).
  - Keep amounts out of titles. Put the merchant and amount in the body, and drop both when "hide amounts" is on.
  - The iPhone's notification service extension renders the text before forwarding. So the option has to apply on both devices, or a custom long look has to redact amounts itself (judgment).
- **Encryption.** `apple-client-stack.md` encrypts payloads and decrypts them in the iPhone notification service extension, a Swift target whatever the phone's UI stack. Watches have their own extension type (watchOS 6+, [Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/usernotifications/unnotificationserviceextension)), but forwarded alerts should arrive already decrypted. **Spike W2.**

### 2.7 Authentication on the watch

- **The passkey API isn't available on watchOS**: `ASAuthorizationPlatformPublicKeyCredentialProvider` lists no watchOS ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/authenticationservices/asauthorizationplatformpublickeycredentialprovider)). On the watch, Apple's options are Sign in with Apple, CloudKit, or custom forms with password AutoFill ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/watchos-apps/authenticating-users-on-apple-watch)).
- **CryptoKit's Secure Enclave API runs on watchOS 6+** ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/cryptokit/secureenclave)). Every Apple Watch from Series 1 has a Secure Enclave ([Platform Security](https://support.apple.com/guide/security/the-secure-enclave-sec59b0b31ff/web)).
- **Relay mode, the MVP, needs no watch credentials.** The watch never calls the server, and the iPhone's own tokens cover the relay (§2.4). Traffic between iPhone and watch is encrypted with keys exchanged at pairing ([Platform Security](https://support.apple.com/guide/security/system-security-for-watchos-secc7d85209d/web)).
- **Enrollment (direct mode, later)** (judgment, mirroring the device model in `apple-client-stack.md`, as a custom grant on the token endpoint per [`native-apps-auth.md`](native-apps-auth.md) §10):
  1. The iPhone app, after Face ID, requests a single-use watch enrollment code with a 5-minute TTL and sends it to the watch over WC.
  2. The watch creates a Secure Enclave P-256 key and redeems the code with its public key, signing a DPoP proof.
  3. It receives a rotating refresh token scoped to `glance:read` and `review:write`. It stores it as `AfterFirstUnlockThisDeviceOnly` in a keychain access group shared with its widget extension, so background refresh and widget reloads can read it (§2.4).
  4. The device can be revoked from any device list. Unpairing also erases the watch ([Platform Security](https://support.apple.com/guide/security/system-security-for-watchos-secc7d85209d/web)).

### 2.8 Privacy on the wrist

- **System lock.** With wrist detection on, the watch "locks automatically soon after it's removed from the user's wrist" ([Platform Security](https://support.apple.com/guide/security/system-security-for-watchos-secc7d85209d/web)). `LAPolicy.deviceOwnerAuthenticationWithWristDetection` (watchOS 9+) fails if no passcode is set or the watch came off the wrist ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/localauthentication/lapolicy/deviceownerauthenticationwithwristdetection)). Proposal: refuse to show amounts if the watch has no passcode, and skip any in-app PIN (judgment).
- **No biometrics.** The biometrics-only policy doesn't exist on watchOS ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/localauthentication/lapolicy/deviceownerauthenticationwithbiometrics)), which leaves passcode-based policies. `PRODUCT.md` says the apps are biometric-locked, so the owner should approve this exception explicitly.
- **Always On in the app.** Apple's guidance is to "always hide any highly sensitive information, such as financial information". Use `privacySensitive()` or `redactionReasons.contains(.privacy)`, and `isLuminanceReduced` to dim content ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/watchos-apps/designing-your-app-for-the-always-on-state); [HIG](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/always-on): "redact … bank balances").
- **Complications.**
  - `privacySensitive` lets "people decide whether a widget should show sensitive data" ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/widgetkit/creating-a-widget-extension)). On watchOS the choice sits in Settings › Clock › Show Data When Locked and Settings › Display & Brightness › Always On › Show Complication Data, and "the settings are `on` by default" (an Apple DTS engineer, Nov 2025, [forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/806236), secondary).
  - Apple's Always On article (2026-09-17) also names Show Complication Data ([Apple Support](https://support.apple.com/en-us/105074)). The WidgetKit page and the [Platform Security guide](https://support.apple.com/guide/security/widgetkit-security-secbb0a1f9b4/web) still describe a "Hide Sensitive Complications" setting, which that article doesn't list. By default, then, amounts marked `privacySensitive` still show.
  - To meet "no amounts in Always On", the complication views swap amounts for gauges when `isLuminanceReduced` is true (same DTS reply).
  - To hide them on a locked watch whatever the setting, add the Data Protection entitlement with `NSFileProtectionComplete` to the widget extension. WidgetKit then "hides widget content when the device is locked" ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/widgetkit/creating-a-widget-extension)), and the extension gets no "runtime while the device is locked" (DTS reply).
  - Watch behavior to confirm in **spike W5**.
- **App option.** Add "Masquer les montants sur la montre" (hide amounts on the watch). It shows gauges and percentages only, in line with the hide-amounts modes in YNAB and Monarch ([`budgeting-products.md`](budgeting-products.md)), and strips amounts from notification text (§2.6).

### 2.9 Storage on the watch

- **What to store:** only `snapshot.json` (a few KB) and `pending.json`, in the app group the watch app shares with its widget extension ([app groups](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/xcode/configuring-app-groups)), plus, in direct mode only, the credentials in a shared keychain access group (§2.7). App groups don't span the iPhone and the watch, which are two devices (judgment).
- **What not to store:** no ledger, history or attachments. Wipe everything on sign-out, revocation, or a `signedOut` context.
- **Quotas:** I found no documented per-app storage quota on watchOS. WC transfers can fail when the target lacks space ([WCSession](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/watchconnectivity/wcsession)).
- **File protection:** set the protection class explicitly. The watchOS default isn't stated in the docs I read.

### 3.1 SwiftUI only, project shape

Use the SwiftUI `App` with `.backgroundTask(.appRefresh)` and `.backgroundTask(.watchConnectivity)` ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/watchkit/using-background-tasks)).
- Navigation follows the watchOS 10 model:
  - a vertical-page `TabView`: Home, then Limits, then Review;
  - `NavigationStack` for detail;
  - toolbars for actions ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/watchos-apps/creating-an-intuitive-and-effective-ui-in-watchos-10)).
- Toolbar and control styles move to Liquid Glass automatically for apps built for watchOS 10+ ([WWDC25-334](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/334/)).
- Series 9+ and Ultra 2 run arm64 on watchOS 26, so keep "Standard Architectures" ([WWDC25-334](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/334/)).
- Since 2026-04-28, uploads must be built with Xcode 26+ and a watchOS 26 SDK ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/news/upcoming-requirements/)).
- TestFlight supports watchOS ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/test-a-beta-version/testflight-overview)).

### 3.3 The phone app isn't native

### 3.4 Sharing code and tokens

```text
Apps/Watch/                SwiftUI watch app (single target, companion of the iOS app)
Extensions/WatchWidgets/   WidgetKit: complications, Smart Stack, relevant widget, controls (later)
Modules/WatchRelay/        Expo module (Swift) in the iPhone app: WCSession, glance fetch, relay client
Extensions/iOS/            notification service extension, iOS widgets
Packages/WatchBridge/      Codable Glance, envelope and WatchAction; cursor rule; WC codec with a version
Packages/APIContract/      glance and push client (the iPhone relay; the watch in direct mode); swift-openapi
Packages/DesignSystem/     generated tokens + MoneyText, BudgetGauge, AccessoryGlance (watch, iOS widgets)
Packages/Security/         Keychain access groups, Secure Enclave, LAPolicy (wrist detection on the watch)
```

- **Where the targets live.** In the committed `ios/` project, for Expo (`cross-platform-ui-stack.md` §4.7) as for Flutter, so the watch targets link these packages. Under CNG they would sit in `/targets`, and while #171 is open their sources would be compiled into each target (judgment). `WatchRelay` lives in the app target; as a local Expo module, how it links `WatchBridge` and `APIContract` depends on Expo's move from CocoaPods to Swift Package Manager ([Expo](https://expo.dev/blog/faster-ios-builds-with-precompiled-xcframeworks)), and compiling their sources in is the fallback (judgment; W7). A Swift server can import `WatchBridge` for the glance types.
- **Package support.** swift-openapi-runtime and -urlsession support watchOS 6+ ([runtime](https://github.com/apple/swift-openapi-runtime), [urlsession](https://github.com/apple/swift-openapi-urlsession)). GRDB 7.12 supports watchOS 7+ and SQLiteData 1.12 watchOS 9+ ([GRDB](https://github.com/groue/GRDB.swift), [SQLiteData](https://github.com/pointfreeco/sqlite-data)). Neither is needed on the watch.
- **One accessory view.** The rectangular and circular families are shared between the iPhone Lock Screen and watch complications ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/widgetkit/creating-accessory-widgets-and-watch-complications)). Write `AccessoryGlance` once, in Swift.
- **Tokens.** Use the single pipeline from [`design-system.md`](design-system.md): Design Tokens Community Group (DTCG) 2025.10 files ([DTCG](https://www.designtokens.org/tr/2025.10/format/)) behind one resolver whose `platform` modifier has a `watchos` context, generated by Terrazzo 2.10.0 ([npm](https://www.npmjs.com/package/@terrazzo/cli)) with in-repo Swift plugins. Style Dictionary 5.8.0 stays a fallback only: "the latest format 2025.10 does not have full support yet" ([docs](https://github.com/style-dictionary/style-dictionary/blob/main/docs/src/content/docs/info/DTCG.mdx)).
- **Icons.** Categories and states use the semantic keys of `icons.yaml`: SF Symbols on Apple, Lucide on the web (§2.4).

### 3.5 Testing

- **Package tests.** Run `swift test` on the `WatchBridge` codec, the cursor rule and the relay client's dedupe, acknowledgment and retry logic, with WC behind a small protocol and an in-memory fake (judgment). Golden JSON fixtures are shared with the server's GlanceBuilder and any phone-side rebuild.
- **UI tests.**
  - swift-snapshot-testing (`factory-ci-quality.md`) compiles its SwiftUI image strategy only under `#if os(iOS) || os(tvOS)` ([source](https://github.com/pointfreeco/swift-snapshot-testing/blob/main/Sources/SnapshotTesting/Snapshotting/SwiftUIView.swift)), and its "Add watchOS support" issue has been open since 2023-02-06 ([#705](https://github.com/pointfreeco/swift-snapshot-testing/issues/705)).
  - So render watch views with `ImageRenderer` (watchOS 9+, [Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/imagerenderer)) and compare images with a small in-repo helper, or take XCUITest screenshots ([`XCUIScreen`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/xcuiautomation/xcuiscreen) lists watchOS), as spike S4 in [`design-system.md`](design-system.md) proposes. Previews stay the state catalog.
  - Render at 40, 44, 46 and 49 mm: the smallest canvas, XLarge default text on a narrow screen, the largest standard case and the Ultra. Add accessibility sizes, Always On (`isLuminanceReduced`, `.privacy`), and accented (tinted) rendering.
  - Sizes run 40/44 (Series 4–6 and every SE), 41/45 (Series 7–9), 42/46 (Series 10 and 11) and 49 mm (every Ultra) ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/watchos-apps/supporting-multiple-watch-sizes)).
- **Paired devices for the main path.** Apple says to "always test Watch Connectivity data transfers on paired devices"; Simulator supports neither `transferUserInfo` nor its delegate callback nor complication transfers ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/watchconnectivity/wcsession/transferuserinfo(_:)), [Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/watchconnectivity/wcsessiondelegate/session(_:didreceiveuserinfo:))), and iOS background tasks need a physical device ([Expo](https://docs.expo.dev/versions/latest/sdk/background-task/)). Notification forwarding and double tap need devices too, and the simulator allows low-level networking that real watches block ([TN3135](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/technotes/tn3135-low-level-networking-on-watchos)). So agents cover the logic in CI, and each release runs a short checklist on a paired watchOS 27 watch and iPhone (judgment).

### 4.2 Components

- **Shared everywhere**, from one spec per component ([`design-system.md`](design-system.md) §5.1): one implementation in the shared UI codebase for iPhone, Mac and web (ADR 0002), and one in SwiftUI for the watch and every WidgetKit surface:
  - `MoneyText`: formatting, sign, currency, redaction;
  - `BudgetGauge`: a ring or a bar;
  - `CategoryBadge`;
  - `StatusLabel`: color, symbol and text;
  - `AccessoryGlance` (SwiftUI only: iPhone Lock Screen widgets and watch complications).
- **Watch-only:** `GlanceHome`, `ReviewCard` (one transaction per screen; suggestions as full-width buttons; Crown to scroll), and `StaleBadge`.
- **Documentation:** the catalog shows a watch variant, or "not on watch", for every component, plus watch previews in the states listed in §3.5.
- **Prototyping:** Apple ships a watchOS 26 UI Kit for Figma and Sketch ([Design Resources](https://developer.apple.com/design/resources/)). Judge glanceability on a real wrist early (judgment).

### 4.3 Localization and regional settings

- **Strings.** String catalogs support plural variants and "Vary by Device", which makes shorter watch strings possible ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/xcode/localizing-and-varying-text-with-a-string-catalog)). "Reste à vivre" is the French term for safe-to-spend ([`budgeting-products.md`](budgeting-products.md)). Inline complications will need a shorter variant.
- **Language.** The watch's language is set in the iPhone's Watch app (General › Language & Region › Custom) and can differ from the iPhone's ([Apple](https://support.apple.com/guide/watch/change-the-language-and-orientation-apd0bf18f46b/watchos)).
- **Formatting.**
  - Send raw values (minor units, ISO currency code, instants, civil dates) and format on the watch with `FormatStyle`. Never send preformatted strings.
  - `prefs` carries the member's stored region, hour cycle and first day of the week, each `null` for "auto" ([`i18n-l10n.md`](i18n-l10n.md) §7, L1).
  - The watch app and its widget extension build the same composite `Locale` as the iPhone, such as `fr-FR-u-fw-mon-hc-h23-rg-dezzzz`, from the bundle's language plus those fields, and set it through `.environment(\.locale, …)` at each root ([`i18n-l10n.md`](i18n-l10n.md) §3.2).
- **Time.**
  - Compute period boundaries in the household's time zone. Send the period end as a civil date with the household zone, as `i18n-l10n.md` does for period bounds (§4; rules T1 and T3 in §7), and derive instants on the watch.
  - Count "J-N" with `Calendar` in that zone, not as multiples of 86,400 s, because of daylight saving time.
  - Apply an explicit 12- or 24-hour choice through `Locale.Components.hourCycle` (watchOS 9+, the BCP 47 `hc` key) ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/foundation/locale/components/hourcycle)), in the app and in the widgets. With "auto", the watch's own setting applies.

## Recommendation for on-budget

### Scope

| Phase | Watch features | Prerequisites | Effort (judgment) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **MVP** | Safe-to-spend home (you and shared, daily budget, J-N, pace), limits page, review inbox (confirm or change, double tap), complications in all 4 families, Smart Stack widget, onboarding that suggests a face complication, forwarded actionable alerts, Always On redaction in the views, hide-amounts option (app, complications, notification text), French and English. Relay mode only: no watch credentials, no WidgetKit push | Phone MVP with `WatchRelay` (WC, native glance fetch, relay client, notification delegate), the native token store and the notification service extension; Tailscale on household iPhones; the sync engine; GlanceBuilder, `GET /v1/glance` and "glance changed" background pushes on the server; alert categories in the iOS app | M–L |
| **Next** | Configurable widget (you or shared). Relevant widget near shops (opt-in location). Alerts on a watch away from its iPhone, if spike W2 shows that identical payloads deduplicate. Direct mode for instances the watch can reach: enrollment, the watch's own glance fetch and outbox, WidgetKit push | MVP; for direct mode, an instance reachable from the internet | M–L |
| **Later** | Cash entry (App Intent, an App Shortcut phrase that names the app, Control); interactive widget (`AccessoryWidgetGroup` with 3 categories), whose intents can only queue actions until the watch app relays them (judgment); goals glance; custom long look with progress; a spending-session Live Activity from iOS; a glance pushed to watches away from their iPhone (§2.2) | MVP | S–M each |
| **Never on the watch** | Allocation, rules, sharing, bank links and consent renewal, splits, reports, account onboarding | — | — |

**MVP acceptance targets** (judgment, checked on a paired watchOS 27 watch and iPhone, since the main path doesn't run in simulators, §3.5):
- the cached glance renders in under 1 s, with its age, in the app and in the rectangular widget;
- with the iPhone in range and Tailscale up, opening the app shows a glance at most 2 min old within 5 s;
- confirming a suggested category takes one tap or a double tap. With the iPhone in range and Tailscale up, the server has it within 10 s; otherwise it shows as pending until the iPhone relays it, and never twice;
- after a bank sync or a partner's edit, a face complication shows the change within 15 min at the median while the iPhone is online (W4);
- with the iPhone away or Tailscale down, nothing fails: the age shows and actions queue;
- no amount appears in Always On, complications included, whatever the watch settings; with "hide amounts" on, no notification shows an amount.

### Decisions

1. Ship a SwiftUI companion watch app and a WidgetKit extension as Swift targets next to the Expo iOS app (ADR 0002), with a watchOS 27.0 floor unless a household watch needs 26.0.
2. Make the server-built glance read model the watch's only data contract, versioned in `WatchBridge` and ordered by one instance-wide cursor.
3. In the MVP, the iPhone relays everything over WC, for every instance. `WatchRelay` fetches the glance natively through the Tailscale tunnel, and a separate native relay client posts the watch's writes and "Confirm" actions. Direct mode comes later, only for instances the watch can reach.
4. Writes reuse named mutations. Alerts target only the iPhone and background actions run there, so in the MVP a watch away from its iPhone gets no alerts.
5. No credentials on the watch in the MVP. The phone-bootstrapped enrollment (§2.7) arrives with direct mode.

### Trade-offs to accept

- **The watch is only as fresh as the iPhone's last relay**, against Apple's advice (TL;DR 3). Mitigations: an "as of" stamp everywhere, a fetch on open, "glance changed" pushes to the iPhone, and onboarding that suggests a face complication. A Smart Stack-only setup gets no complication transfers and may refresh once a day, and force-quitting the iPhone app stops background pushes until it is reopened (§2.3).
- **The iPhone carries native Swift beside the TypeScript UI:** `WatchRelay`, a second sync client in Swift (outbox, HLC, retries), the `Security` module, the notification service extension and the widgets. The committed `ios/` project takes a hand-applied native diff at every Expo SDK upgrade (`cross-platform-ui-stack.md` §4.7).
- **Tailscale must stay up on the iPhone.** Another VPN or a disconnecting On Demand rule stalls the watch, so the iPhone app warns (§2.4).
- **Budgets are the system's call.** No mechanism guarantees freshness: background tasks, WC deliveries, WidgetKit reloads and pushes are all opportunistic.
- **No alerts without the iPhone** in the MVP (§2.6).
- **Device testing.** The main path, WC transfers, background wakes and notification forwarding, runs only on paired devices, so agents can't verify it in CI. Watch UI images need an in-repo helper (§3.5).
- **Older watches.** A 27.0 floor leaves out Series 6–8, SE 2 and Ultra 1.
- **Only the dark theme on the watch.** The light theme stays phone-, Mac- and web-only.
- **No biometrics on the watch.** Its lock is the passcode plus wrist detection, an exception to `PRODUCT.md`'s biometric lock that the owner must approve.
- **Self-hosting and APNs.** Each APNs request is signed with a key from the team that owns the app's topic ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/usernotifications/establishing-a-token-based-connection-to-apns)). If a self-hoster installs a build signed by another team, their server can't send alerts or "glance changed" pushes to it, and `PRODUCT.md` leaves open how self-hosters get the native apps. Without push, the relay falls back to the iPhone's `BGAppRefreshTask` and to opening the watch app (judgment).

## Open questions and spikes

**Spikes** (each at most one day, on real devices):

**Questions for the owner:**

- Which watches do the household's members wear? Series 9+, SE 3 and Ultra 2+ allow the watchOS 27.0 floor; an older one needs 26.0 and its complications stay stale.
- Do you accept that, on the Tailscale-only instance, the watch is only as fresh as the iPhone's last relay, with its age shown?
- Should household iPhones keep Tailscale's automatic On Demand policy, with no rule that disconnects it?
- Should the watch later talk to the server directly on instances that self-hosters expose publicly (direct mode)?
- Do you accept passcode plus wrist detection on the watch in place of the biometric lock in `PRODUCT.md`?
- On the watch face, should amounts be visible by default when the watch is awake, or should the watch show only gauges by default?
- Should alerts show the merchant and amount by default, or generic text?
- Should the default glance show your own money, shared money, or both?
- Should Smart Stack relevance near shops be allowed, given it needs location permission?
- Can a watch confirm categories on shared transactions, or only on your own?
- Should opening the watch app trigger a bank refresh with the user present (`bank-aggregation.md`, "user present")? I propose no, because a watch session lasts seconds.
- How will self-hosters get the native apps? That decides whether their servers can push alerts and "glance changed" hints to them.
