Research date: 2026-11-07. Prices and features are as of Nov 2026 unless dated otherwise (list prices, before tax).
"Secondary" marks reviews, forums, press and App Store user reviews, used only to locate sources or report recurring complaints.
Confidence: **H** high, **M** medium, **L** low. Bank-data providers: [`bank-aggregation.md`](bank-aggregation.md). Client stack: [`apple-client-stack.md`](apple-client-stack.md).

## Question

Which budgeting methods, product features, behavioral mechanisms and Apple-platform UX patterns should shape `on-budget`? The target: a household in France, typically a couple (personal accounts, joint account optional), whose banks are the supported banks in `PRODUCT.md` (BoursoBank, Crédit Agricole Aquitaine, Crédit Mutuel du Sud-Ouest). It plans easily, anticipates recurring flows, sets limits, gets alerts, funds goals and stays accountable, with little manual work, every day on iPhone and Apple Watch, and plans on the Mac or the full-parity web app.

Covered: the r/budget "Budgeting 101" wiki and the main methods. Products: YNAB, Monarch, Copilot, Lunch Money, Actual, PocketSmith, Rocket Money, Simplifi, Goodbudget, Buddy, Emma, Finary, Bankin', Linxo, Kresus, plus two 2025 entrants (Finzee, Linxo Lab) and Honeydue (couples reference). Then behavioral evidence, UX patterns and pitfalls. Not covered: picking a bank-data provider (see `bank-aggregation.md`).

## TL;DR

1. **No existing app fits the brief; Actual comes closest.** Actual is open source, self-hosted and local-first, with optional end-to-end encryption, envelope budgeting, OpenID multi-user and an iOS app in beta. But it has no Watch app, no per-person ownership inside a budget, and one Enable Banking credential set per server, where `PRODUCT.md` wants one per person. The US leaders take only US/Canadian banks (Monarch, Copilot, Simplifi, Rocket Money, Honeydue); YNAB runs as a web app on computers and imports European banks one by one via Plaid. French apps with sync plan lightly at best (Bankin': category limits and a pace-based month-end forecast; Linxo Lab: a monthly "reste à vivre"); none allocates every euro or splits shared money per person (Finzee does zero-based, without sync). Of them, only Finary confirms Crédit Mutuel du Sud-Ouest; Bankin' and Linxo name only "Crédit Mutuel", and Kresus's CMSO module is stale. On the Mac only Copilot is native; Finary, Buddy, Emma and Rocket Money run there as mobile apps. YNAB, Buddy and Linxo have Watch apps; Monarch, Copilot, Finary and Bankin' do not. The gap to own: self-hosted, per-person French bank data, iPhone, Watch, Mac and offline web, configurable couple sharing, a plan-first loop. **H**
2. **Each of the four mechanisms confirmed in `PRODUCT.md` already ships somewhere, but no app combines them.** Zero-based allocation: YNAB, Actual, Finzee. Safe to spend: Copilot "Free to Spend" (budget left this month; its chart leaves out recurrings due this month) with an ideal-pace line; Simplifi's "left to spend per day for the rest of the month", the nearest match to the daily budget; Monarch's Flex bar, yellow when spending outpaces the month; PocketSmith's "Safe Balance" (beta), an account balance minus unspent budgets; a "reste à vivre" in Finzee and Linxo Lab. Category limits: all of them. Review inbox: YNAB Card Mode, Copilot "To Review", Monarch "Needs review". Monarch's Flex model (fixed, non-monthly, one flexible number) shows how to keep allocation light. **H** on the facts, **M** that the combination works.
3. **For couples, the unmet need is attribution inside the plan, and privacy.** Monarch already puts a Shared or Individual owner on every account and transaction, filters Accounts, Transactions, Reports and Cash Flow by owner, and can split a transaction by rule with an owner per split. But its Budgets, Recurring and Goals always show the whole household, and no account can be private. Copilot has no households yet; Bankin' has no shared space for two logins. Finary applies a joint account's ownership split to net worth but not to the budget, and users have asked for that since Feb 2024. Honeydue lets each partner share an account fully, as balances only, or not at all. Carry the owner (me, partner, shared) into the plan: per-person budgets and safe to spend, a split rule for shared money, and per-account visibility. **H** on the gap, **M** on the design.
4. **Daily use comes from a short loop more than from features**: inbox, then safe-to-spend, a weekly recap and a monthly check-in. The evidence: monitoring progress raises goal attainment, more so when progress is recorded (Harkin 2016). Reminders raise saving, more so when they name the goal (Karlan 2016). Goal setting inside an app raised savings (Gargano & Rossi 2024). A finance app's release cut insufficient-funds (NSF) fees (Carlin 2023). **M–H**
5. **Design against abandonment.** People stop tracking because of the hassle, discomfort or guilt, and unreliable data (Epstein 2016). Broken streaks lower later engagement (Silverman & Barasch 2023). Card-payment reminders raised overdraft fees for some users (Medina 2021). So: automate first, keep the tone neutral, avoid loss-framed streaks, and base every alert on all accounts. **M**
6. **Bank data is the main dependency.** GoCardless Bank Account Data stopped taking new sign-ups in Jul 2025, consents expire (at least every 180 days under PSD2) and every app with bank sync documents broken connections. Plan reconnect flows and file import from day one. Aggregators treat Crédit Mutuel du Sud-Ouest as its own connector, apart from Crédit Mutuel, so test it on its own. FinanceKit is US/UK only. **H**
7. **Joint accounts are optional and may come later (`PRODUCT.md`).** In a randomized study, couples told to open one kept relationship quality that the others lost over two years (Olson et al. 2023). Pooling money also correlates with satisfaction (Gladstone et al. 2022, N = 38,534). So make adding a joint account later easy, without forcing one. **M**

## Budgeting fundamentals

### r/budget wiki "Budgeting 101"

- **Access:** www and old.reddit.com answer "blocked by network security", so I did not retry them. I read Wayback snapshots from 2025-08-09: the [index](https://web.archive.org/web/20250809191246/https://www.reddit.com/r/budget/wiki/budgting101/) and subpage C, [first budget](https://web.archive.org/web/20250809191220/https://www.reddit.com/r/budget/wiki/budgting101/how-to-create-your-first-budget/). Subpages A, B and D are not archived, so they are unverified.
- **Structure:** (A) track income and spending to get "the full picture"; (B) methods "like the 50/30/20 Rule, Zero-Based Budgeting, the Envelope System, and more"; (C) the first budget, step by step; (D) common pitfalls.
- **C, step by step:**
  1. *Choose a method* without chasing a perfect one: 50/30/20 "for simple guidelines", zero-based "for total control", "traditional" "for a structured plan with limits".
  2. *Draft the plan:* income at the top, categories from your tracking, then *planned* amounts. Example: groceries go from $500 to $450 and the freed $50 gets "a new job" (debt or a sinking fund). The wiki calls this "active budgeting".
  3. *Balance it:* in zero-based, income minus planned spending equals 0; in traditional or 50/30/20, it is at least 0. If the plan doesn't fit, cut.
  4. *Prepare the month:* a new spreadsheet tab with the limits, or cash in envelopes.
- **For on-budget:** the wiki's track, choose, plan, balance, run sequence can serve as the onboarding script. Import history first, then propose a plan from it, as Monarch does when it prefills budgets from the last 6 months ([Monarch](https://help.monarch.com/hc/en-us/articles/32125337244052)) and Bankin' from 3-month averages ([Bankin'](https://support.bankin.com/hc/fr/articles/20540496127889)).

### Emergency fund and sinking funds in France

- **Sizing.** Banque de France's education site: "2 à 6 mois de revenus et plus généralement à 3 mois". Counting months of essential spending (rent, insurance, subscriptions, food) instead is "moins protecteur et n'est pas forcement adapté à toutes les personnes" ([Mes questions d'argent](https://www.mesquestionsdargent.fr/epargne-et-placements/epargne-de-precaution)). Crédit Agricole uses spending: 3–6 months of "dépenses courantes". Three months suits a permanent employee who rents with no dependents; six suits the self-employed, mortgage holders and families. Keep it in liquid savings: Livret A, LDDS, LEP ([Crédit Agricole](https://www.credit-agricole.fr/particulier/conseils/magazine/tout-un-mag/epargne-de-precaution-quel-montant-ou-placer.html)). **H**
- **For on-budget:** propose a target in months of the household's own income (3 by default, per Banque de France) or of all its current spending (Crédit Agricole's 3–6), not of essential spending alone, and show "months covered". That is clearer than YNAB's Age of Money (the average age of the last 10 cash outflows, [YNAB](https://support.ynab.com/en_us/age-of-money-H1ZS84W1s)).

### Couples: "yours, mine, ours"

- **YNAB:** couples have "three sets of priorities: yours, mine, and ours". It recommends a regular money meeting and personal "fun money" for each partner. It describes three set-ups: all accounts shared, all separate, or hybrid. The hybrid comes in two variants: (A) two personal plans plus one shared plan; (B) one plan with category groups per person, where payments into the joint account are transfers ([YNAB guide](https://www.ynab.com/guide/budgeting-as-a-couple)).
- **Monarch:** one shared budget per household; "You cannot hide bank accounts or transactions" ([Monarch](https://help.monarch.com/hc/en-us/articles/20926382202004)).
- **Honeydue:** for each account, a partner chooses "both balances and transactions", "balances only" or "nothing at all" ([Honeydue](https://support.honeydue.com/en/articles/3179542-will-my-partner-see-my-bank-account-balances-and-transactions)).
- **No joint account** (optional in `PRODUCT.md`): shared costs are paid from personal accounts, so they need splitting and settling. Buddy shows "who paid what, and how to balance" ([App Store FR](https://apps.apple.com/fr/app/id936422955)). YNAB documents a Splitwise workflow ([index](https://support.ynab.com/llms.txt)).
- **Fit by method:** zero-based and flex both work if every account and transaction carries an owner. 50/30/20 and the anti-budget work only at household level. Forecasting matters most for a joint account, which both partners draw on. **M**

## Behavioral design

| Mechanism | Evidence | In products | Implication for on-budget |
|---|---|---|---|
| Recorded progress monitoring | Meta-analysis of 138 randomized studies (N = 19,951): interventions raised monitoring frequency (d = 1.98) and goal attainment, with larger effects when progress was reported or physically recorded ([Harkin et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1037/bul0000025)). **H** (mostly health studies) | YNAB approval, Copilot "To Review", Monarch "Needs review" | A daily inbox that "records" each transaction; show progress, not just balances |
| Easier access to one's own data | After the Meniga app launched in Iceland, NSF fees fell ([Carlin, Olafsson & Pagel 2023](https://doi.org/10.1093/rof/rfac040)). Mobile access brought more logins at retail peak hours and less discretionary spending (working paper, [Levi & Benartzi](https://hhs.se/globalassets/swedish-house-of-finance/conference/annual-conference-2020/ma1.pdf)). **M** | All | Fast launch, widgets, safe-to-spend visible at a glance |
| Goals and earmarking | Goal setting in a budgeting app raised saving without crowding out other saving; the channels were monitoring and concreteness ([Gargano & Rossi 2024](https://doi.org/10.1111/jofi.13339)). Splitting earmarked cash into separate envelopes increased saving ([Soman & Cheema 2011](https://www.russellsage.org/sites/default/files/u137/jmr-C-s014-s021-online.pdf)). **H** | Monarch Goals, YNAB targets, Finzee | Named goals and sinking funds as separate, visible pots |
| Mental budgets curb spending | Category budgets lead people to buy less in the budgeted categories ([Heath & Soll 1996](https://doi.org/10.1086/209465)). **M** | Every app with limits | Put limits on discretionary categories only, where restraint is wanted |
| Reminders and nudges | Reminders raised saving; messages naming the goal worked best ([Karlan et al. 2016](https://www.nber.org/papers/w16205)). Card-payment reminders cut late fees by ~14 % but raised overdraft fees by ~9 % ([Medina 2021](https://doi.org/10.1093/rfs/hhaa108)). **H** | Copilot Monday update, Monarch recap | Name the goal; check all accounts before nudging (no "pay your card" nudge that overdraws the checking account) |
| Spending pace | No study found on pace lines. **L** | Copilot ideal-pace line; Monarch's yellow Flex bar; Bankin's pace-based forecast | Use it, but test whether people understand it |
| Fresh starts | Temporal landmarks (new week, month, year) trigger aspirational behavior ([Dai, Milkman & Riis 2014](https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.2014.1901)). **H** | YNAB "Fresh Start", monthly rollover | Ritual at each month start, as income arrives; "start over" without shame |
| Ostrich effect | Investors log in 9.5 % less after market declines ([Sicherman et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1093/rfs/hhv073)). **H** | – | Don't wait for users to pull bad news; deliver it gently with a next step |
| Streaks | A logged streak that breaks lowers later engagement, less so when it can be repaired ([Silverman & Barasch 2023](https://doi.org/10.1093/jcr/ucac029)). **M** | Rare in finance apps | At most a repairable "weeks reviewed" count; no loss framing |
| Friction / pain of paying | Willingness to pay rose when buyers had to pay by credit card instead of cash ([Prelec & Simester 2001](https://doi.org/10.1023/A:1008196717017)). **M** | YNAB's approval step; Goodbudget's manual envelopes | Keep the small friction of confirming in the inbox; don't add friction to logging |
| Tone and personality | Copilot sells assistant voices from "Zen" to "Roast me" ([site](https://www.copilot.money/)); no outcome data. **L** | Copilot | Neutral by default; Epstein's quotes below show guilt drives quitting |
| Couples pooling | 230 couples, 6 waves: those assigned to a joint account kept relationship quality that the others lost ([Olson et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1093/jcr/ucad020); 20 % attrition). Pooling all money goes with higher satisfaction, most among strained couples ([Gladstone et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1037/pspi0000388)). **M** | YNAB Together, Monarch households | Make "ours" first-class and keep personal autonomy |
| Vendor outcome claims | YNAB: "new YNABers save $600 in their first month… more than $6,000 after one year" ([pricing](https://www.ynab.com/pricing)). Marketing, not evidence. **L** | – | Don't copy such claims |

## UX patterns and pitfalls

### Patterns worth emulating

- **Navigation (iPhone).** Four or five tabs; YNAB's are Home, Plan, Spending, Accounts and Reflect, with "Add Transaction" on each. Copilot opens on its dashboard and lets users reorder the other tabs ([help](https://help.copilot.money/en/articles/6045480-dashboard-tab-overview)). Apple's guidance: tab bars are for navigation, not actions; fewer tabs are easier; avoid overflow tabs ([HIG](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/tab-bars)).
- **Navigation (Mac).** Sidebar with customizable sections plus keyboard shortcuts and fast search: Copilot's ⌘1, ⌘F and ⌘R, Monarch's ⌘K command bar. Sidebars float in Liquid Glass on macOS 26 ([HIG](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/sidebars), [Apple](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2025/06/apple-introduces-a-delightful-and-elegant-new-software-design/)). Standard SwiftUI components inherit this, per `apple-client-stack.md`; whether the shared UI codebase required by `PRODUCT.md` does depends on the stack (`cross-platform-ui-stack.md`).
- **Transaction review.**
  - A count on Home opens a card stack: swipe right to approve, swipe left to skip, undo within the session, then a pass over skipped items (YNAB Card Mode, iOS and Android). Monarch's mobile review swipes right or left from a dashboard banner, with no undo documented; Goodbudget only swipes right to confirm auto-assigned imports on iPhone.
  - The app learns from every edit: Monarch offers a quick rule, YNAB remembers the category per payee, and Copilot puts its top two guesses first.
  - For shared money, ask "whose is this?" first; Monarch lets you pick who should review a transaction.
- **Budget visualization.** A "left to spend" headline with an ideal-pace line (Copilot). A per-day amount for the rest of the month (Simplifi). A Flex bar that turns yellow when spending outpaces the month (Monarch). Rings and progress bars (Monarch's widget, YNAB). A recurring calendar colored paid, different amount, upcoming, missed (Monarch). Flex categories sorted by amount spent (Monarch). Sankey cash flow (Monarch, PocketSmith, Finary).
- **Notification cadence.** Copilot uses thresholds plus one weekly digest, with no push per transaction. YNAB mostly prompts actions, plus occasional messages such as a trial ending. Monarch sends a recap and recurring reviews; Linxo announces salary arrival. Apple's guidance ([HIG](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/notifications), [managing](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/managing-notifications)):
  - Don't send several notifications for the same thing, or people "may turn off all notifications from your app".
  - Keep "sensitive, personal, or confidential information" out of notifications.
  - Use interruption levels: Passive, Active, or Time Sensitive (only for events "happening now or will happen within an hour").
  - Badges only count unread notifications; marketing pushes need explicit consent.
- **Glanceable surfaces.** Widgets should show "dynamic information that changes throughout the day" ([HIG](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/widgets)). Monarch's widgets cover budget, transactions to review, investments and receipt capture. YNAB also offers Siri, Spotlight and Shortcuts. "Hide amounts" modes exist in YNAB and in Monarch's widgets.
- **Onboarding.** Prefill from history (Monarch: 6 months; Bankin': 3-month averages; Copilot: the assistant drafts a budget). Start small (YNAB: checking plus one card). Offer a guided walkthrough (Monarch).

### Pitfalls that make people quit

1. **Broken or stale sync.** Monarch documents credential-based connections that break, banks that force reconnects, and providers that drop banks ([help](https://help.monarch.com/hc/en-us/articles/33707613533972)). PSD2 consent must be renewed at least every 180 days ([RTS](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg_del/2018/389/2023-07-25/eng)), and every 90 days at most via Enable Banking in Actual. Show warnings before expiry, and offer file import. **H**
2. **Tedium.** "I got behind on keeping up with it and couldn't find the time to start back up"; "I got burnt out on [keeping a financial spreadsheet]" ([Epstein et al. 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5428074/)). **H**
3. **Guilt and discomfort.** Users "didn't like being so aware of how little money I had", "felt guilty every time I tracked an expense that was not a necessity" (same study). **H**
4. **Wrong numbers.** Missing splits and refunds (Bankin' reviews, secondary), joint spending counted at 100 % for each partner (Finary forum, secondary), and duplicates when both partners link the same joint account (Copilot's own warning). **M**
5. **One-person design.** Copilot's partners share one login; Monarch cannot keep an account private; Bankin' offers no shared space for two logins ([help](https://support.bankin.com/hc/fr/articles/50711231770769)). **M**
6. **Wrong locale.** USD-only apps (Monarch, Copilot) cannot handle euros. The French term "reste à vivre" (Finzee, Linxo Lab) is the local word for safe-to-spend. **H**
7. **Notification overload** leads people to switch off all of an app's notifications (Apple HIG, above). **M**
8. **Instability.** Features removed without notice (an unlinked Bankin' review, unverified) and price changes; Copilot keeps "legacy pricing" only for uninterrupted subscriptions ([help](https://help.copilot.money/en/articles/6860727-managing-your-copilot-subscription)). **L**

## Recommended feature set for on-budget

Positioning against this research: open source and self-hosted (as Actual is), French banks with per-person credentials, iPhone, Apple Watch, Mac and a full-parity offline web app, couple sharing you configure, and one loop (monthly allocation, safe to spend with a daily budget, pace limits, review inbox). No product above combines these.

### MVP (the owner and partner use it daily)

1. **French bank import plus fallbacks**: the supported banks in `PRODUCT.md` (BoursoBank, Crédit Agricole Aquitaine, Crédit Mutuel du Sud-Ouest) through the provider chosen in `bank-aggregation.md`, with each member's own provider credentials (not Actual's one set per server), plus OFX/CSV import and fast manual entry. Test Crédit Mutuel du Sud-Ouest on its own: aggregators list it apart from Crédit Mutuel, and woob's `cmso` module is stale. *Core need; when sync breaks, the numbers stay trustworthy.*
2. **Ownership on every account and transaction** (me, partner, shared), with a split rule for shared items (50/50, a fixed %, or proportional to income) that feeds each person's budgets and safe to spend. *Configurable sharing. Monarch's owners stop at accounts, transactions and reports: its Budgets, Recurring and Goals stay household-wide and no account can be private. Finary splits joint accounts in net worth only.*
3. **Per-account visibility for the partner**: everything, balances only, or hidden (Honeydue's model). *"Private by default, shared by choice."*
4. **Monthly allocation**: plans run on calendar months (`PRODUCT.md`). As income arrives, every euro goes to *fixed* costs (from recurring items), *sinking funds* (target, frequency, rollover), *goals*, or *flexible* spending; each category's rollover rule (carry over, move to a goal, reset) handles what is left at month end. *Zero-based as confirmed, kept light the way Monarch's Flex is.*
5. **Safe to spend, shown two ways**: the amount left for flexible spending in the period, and a daily budget (that amount divided by the days left), for each person and for the shared budget, with an ideal-pace line on Home. Counting to month end or to the next payday is still open in `PRODUCT.md`. *The number the owner checks daily; Copilot's pace line, Simplifi's per-day figure.*
6. **Limits with two alerts**: a threshold (e.g. 80 % and 100 %) and pace (ahead of the ideal line by X %). *The confirmed "threshold and pace" requirement.*
7. **Recurring detection and a bills calendar** (paid, different amount, upcoming, missed) plus a projected end-of-period balance for each account. *Anticipation and overdraft avoidance, as in Monarch, Linxo and Bankin'.*
8. **Review inbox**: swipe to confirm the category and owner, rules learned from edits, splits, refunds linked to their purchase. *Semi-automatic accuracy, plus the daily ritual.*
9. **Goals**: an emergency fund (target in months of income, 3 by default per Banque de France, or of current spending; shown as "months covered") and big purchases, each with a monthly amount, on-track status and a linked savings account (Livret A, etc.). *The owner's stated goals, backed by the goal-setting evidence.*
10. **Few, well-ranked notifications**: projected overdraft (Time Sensitive), limit or pace crossed (Active), bill amount changed, salary received, consent expiring, and a Monday recap (Passive). No amounts on the lock screen by default. *Alerts without fatigue.*
11. **One UI codebase for the iOS, macOS and web apps, plus a native SwiftUI Watch app** (`PRODUCT.md`). The web app matches the native apps, offline included. The Watch serves the daily short session: what is left to spend, new transactions to review (its scope is still open). Face ID / Touch ID lock, a hide-amounts mode, French and English UI, euros. *Daily use on iPhone and Watch, planning on the Mac or the web; privacy.*

### v1

- **Widgets** (safe-to-spend, inbox count, a goal), App Intents and Shortcuts (log a cash expense), Siri. *More glanceable touchpoints.*
- **Monthly couple check-in**: review the period, plan the next one, adjust goals. *A fresh-start ritual and the "money meeting" YNAB recommends.*
- **"Who owes whom" settle-up** for shared costs paid from personal accounts, plus a contribution planner for a future joint account. *Joint accounts are optional in `PRODUCT.md`; this serves households without one and eases adding one.*
- **Forecast 3–12 months ahead with what-if** (e.g. the date of a big purchase), as Simplifi and PocketSmith do. *Anticipation beyond one period.*
- **Reports**: by category and owner, trends, net worth with manual savings balances. *Accountability over time.*
- **Notes on transactions and questions to the partner.** *The couple's conversation stays attached to the data.*
- **Cleanup of French bank labels** ("PRLV SEPA", "CB … 12/10"). *Readable inbox.*

### Later

- Investment tracking (PEA, assurance-vie). *Finary does this well; not core to budgeting.*
- AI questions and suggestions (Monarch, Copilot). *Useful once the data model is solid; never acts without approval.*
- Receipt capture, multiple currencies for travel, export and API.
- Shared budgets for trips with friends (Buddy-style). *Outside the household use case.*

### Won't do

- Bill negotiation, subscription-cancellation help, credit scores. *US-centric services with heavy operations.*
- Ads and affiliate "coach" offers (Bankin'). *They erode trust in a private app.*
- Payments and transfers. *Regulated scope with little benefit.*
- Loss-framed streaks, leaderboards, shaming copy. *Evidence links guilt and broken streaks to quitting.*
- FinanceKit import. *US and UK only ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/financekit/)).*
- A native Android app. *Not among the apps in `PRODUCT.md`; the full-parity web app serves other devices.*

## Open questions

1. **Period and allocation**: `PRODUCT.md` settles that plans run on calendar months and that each category has its own rule for unspent money. Still open: how a salary paid late in the month (or two different paydays in a couple) funds the next month's plan, and each category type's default rule.
2. **Safe-to-spend definition**: `PRODUCT.md` shows it two ways (flexible money left, and a daily budget). Still open: does it count to month end or to the next payday? Are bills not yet debited subtracted? Is there one figure per person, one shared, or both?
3. **Sharing model**: one household plan with owner labels (Monarch-like), or personal plans plus a shared plan (YNAB 3A)? Can a partner edit transactions on my accounts?
4. **Defaults**: is a new account private, balances only, or shared? Can a partner see a private account exists?
5. **Shared-money maths**: how does a shared expense count in "my" view (50/50, a %, by contribution)? Is money paid into a future joint account a contribution (counts as my spending) or a neutral transfer?
6. **Limits**: which categories get limits by default? How much ahead of pace triggers an alert, and how often at most?
7. **Review ritual**: daily or weekly by default? Confirm everything, or auto-accept confident categorizations?
8. **Notifications**: which ones are on by default; lock-screen privacy; quiet hours.
9. **Goals**: does the money for a goal live in a real savings account (balance-linked) or as a virtual allocation inside the checking account?
10. **Language and tone**: French UI with French terms ("reste à vivre", "épargne de précaution")? Neutral assistant voice or none?
11. **Friends and family (v1)**: same defaults for every household, or a choice of setup at onboarding (solo, couple without joint account, couple with joint account)?
